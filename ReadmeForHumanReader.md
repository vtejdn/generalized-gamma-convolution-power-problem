This repository records an experiment in using AI to explore a mathematical research problem.

We targeted Bondesson's conjecture on generalized gamma convolutions: if a random variable \(X\) is a GGC and \(q\ge 1\), is the power \(X^q\) again a GGC? Working with an AI assistant, we built a proof ledger, tested routes, and arrived at a candidate proof recorded in the ledger and manuscript.

The value of the experiment is twofold: it helps us learn how to use AI to sustain a long, multi-stage mathematical investigation, and it provides an auditable research trail, preserving mathematical derivations, computational checks, and unsuccessful approaches for human scrutiny.
The result has not been peer-reviewed or formally verified in Lean; the repository is offered as a transparent case study in AI-assisted mathematics.

After learning that another researcher had independently developed a similar candidate proof, we decided to make a [preliminary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26558) available on arXiv before completing our own independent verification.
We will continue to check and deepen our understanding of the argument, reorganize and distill its mathematical ideas, and rewrite the manuscript accordingly.
We will document our progress in this repository and post revised versions of the manuscript on arXiv.
Lean formalization of the candidate proof is currently underway in this repository.

As a separate personal experiment, the repository owner is developing a framework for autonomous and collaborative AI-assisted mathematical research, to be released when ready.
We will also share our experiences of using this framework for AI-assisted mathematical exploration.
